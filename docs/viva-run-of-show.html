The run of show

Eight segments. The order is the argument — a rider needs a scooter → here is the city → here is what we predict → here is what we simulate → here is what we optimise → and here is us acting on what the simulation found. It ends by closing that loop, which is the strongest thirty seconds you have.

0:00 The problemno screen · look at them 90s
“A city planner in Belagavi has to decide where to put forty docking stations, or whether a bike lane on Congress Road actually reduces congestion. Today that gets decided on intuition and tested by building it — and if it's wrong, the money is spent and the road is dug up. This is a virtual copy of the city where you test the policy before you build it.”

Then one line on what makes it more than a dashboard: “Anyone can plot scooters on a map. The point here is the counterfactual — run the same city twice, once with your policy and once without, same random seed, and diff the results.”

1:30 Sign upthe sign-in page 1 min
  • Click Create an account. Fill it in live — any email, password at least 10 characters with a letter and a digit.
  • Press Create account & view the map. You land signed in, on the live city.
“A new account is a commuter — they can see the city and plan journeys. The planning and operations screens need a role an administrator grants. That's enforced on the server, not by hiding buttons.”

Doing this live rather than logging in with a prepared account is worth the thirty seconds: it proves registration, session handling and role assignment in one action.

2:30 Plan a journeyPlan · the rider's story 3 min
  • Press Sample trip. It drops both pins instantly — do not hunt around the map in front of an examiner.
  • Three routes appear on their own. Click each card in turn.
  • Point at a green figure: that route wins on that measure.
  • Scroll down: Available near your pickup — real vehicles, walking distance, battery.
  • Scroll further: Charging on the way — stations within 700 m of the route you chose.
“Three routes over the same real street graph, weighted differently. Fastest tolerates traffic and hills. Safest prefers lit streets and bike lanes and costs you minutes. The trade-off is the product — and this is A-star search with a multi-objective cost function, running in a separate Python service.”
If they ask how the route knows where you clicked

Say the honest thing: routes must start and end on an actual road, so a pin is snapped to the nearest one — and the screen tells you how far it moved. It is adjusting your input and admitting it, rather than silently routing from somewhere else.

5:30 Datawhere the machine learning is 2 min

This answers "where's the AI?" before it is asked.

  • Headline tiles: model error against the baseline, and the skill ratio.
  • The forecast chart — point at the shaded band.
  • The model registry: every version, its metrics, which one is active.
“LightGBM forecasting trips per zone per fifteen minutes. Error 0.242 against a seasonal-naive baseline of 0.610 — a skill ratio of 0.397, where the design document asked for 0.70 or better. The split is chronological, not random, and the harness refuses a split where validation starts before training ends. A random split on a time series lets the model see Tuesday evening while predicting Tuesday morning.”

If they lean in: the shaded band is the 80% interval, and a model with no recorded evaluation never gets the active flag.

7:30 Simthe centrepiece — protect this time 3 min 30s
  • Four scenarios. Baseline is today's city; each other one changes exactly one thing.
  • Select Protected bike lanes, press Run simulation. It finishes in about 200 ms.
  • Read the tiles, then the line saying why demand went unmet.
  • Compare against → Baseline.
“Protected bike lanes cut the incident rate 31% while slightly increasing trips served. And the seed line — same seed, same scenario, identical metrics, with a test asserting exactly that. A simulation you cannot reproduce isn't evidence.”
The finding to plant here — you use it again at 13:00

Every unmet trip in the baseline reads “no charged vehicle” — not “not enough scooters”.

“The answer this gives a planner isn't buy more vehicles. It's build charging. Nobody coded that conclusion — it came out of the simulation. Hold that thought, I'll come back to it.”

11:00 Fleet → Rebalancingthe optimisation 2 min
  • Read the table: what each zone has against its target.
  • Press Solve rebalancing. Six vehicles, ~20 km, imbalance 7.8 → 1.8.
  • Drag worth driving N km to 1 and solve again — the plan empties and says why.
“A min-cost transportation problem, solved by linear programming. The target is each zone's demand-weighted share of the fleet that exists — rebalancing can't create vehicles, it decides where the ones you have should stand, so the gaps sum to zero. And it returns whole vehicles with no rounding, because a transportation problem's constraint matrix is totally unimodular.”

That last clause is the most mathematically substantial sentence in the demo, and it is true. Worth memorising exactly.

13:00 Admin — and close the loopAnalytics, then Charging 1 min 30s
  • Analytics: 30-day KPIs, then point at Where vehicles accumulate — Shahapur −1,253, Sadashiv Nagar +936.
  • Switch to the Charging tab. Click the map in a thin residential area, name it, press Add station.
“This chart and the rebalancing planner are the same problem from opposite ends — vehicles draining out of Shahapur overnight, and the optimiser that moves them back. And here I'm acting on what the simulation told us earlier: it said the constraint was charge, not fleet size. So I place a charging station where cover is thin. The simulator found the problem, and this is the tool that responds to it.”
Why this ending works

You have just shown the system produce a finding and then act on it. Most student projects show features side by side. This shows one feeding another — which is what an integrated system means.

14:30 GBFS, and stopa pre-opened browser tab 30s

Switch to a tab already showing 127.0.0.1:4000/gbfs/2.3/free_bike_status.json, with no login.

“A public GBFS feed — the open standard, so any journey planner could consume this fleet without integrating against our private API. And what's deliberately not in it: only available vehicles, because an in-use vehicle's position is a rider's live location. The IDs rotate hourly so nobody polling can follow one vehicle across days.”

One number to be careful with

If you claim the min-cost solver beats the greedy one it replaced — −18.4% unserved for 78 van-km versus 13.0% for 204 km — say that this was measured on a deliberately thin fleet where rebalancing bites hardest. Questions they will ask

Can a user actually book a scooter?

No — and say so without flinching. “This is the planning layer beneath a rental service, not the service itself. You can plan a journey and see which vehicles are available with their battery levels, but not unlock one. A booking flow means payments, and no card data touches this system by design — a half-built payment path is a liability, not a feature.”

Where is the AI in this?

Two places, and be precise about which is which. LightGBM forecasting demand per zone per fifteen minutes, evaluated against a baseline with a chronological split. And A\* search with a multi-objective cost function for routing. The rebalancing solver is optimisation, not learning — say that rather than overselling it.

Is any of this real data?

Volunteer this before they ask. The street network is real — genuine OpenStreetMap geometry for Belagavi. Trips and telemetry are synthetic, generated with realistic daily and weekly patterns, because real rider data is personal and we do not have it. Offered freely it reads as rigour; extracted under questioning it reads as spin.

Why doesn't the simulation route each rider street by street?

A deliberate choice, written up as an architecture decision record. Riders travel zone to zone at a congestion-adjusted speed. Routing 30,000 riders through A\* would take minutes instead of 200 ms and would not move the metrics a planner acts on — wait time and unmet demand are set by where the vehicles are, not which street was taken.

Why is the incident count a decimal?

Because it is an expected value, not a count. At realistic injury rates a 24-hour run samples zero or one incident, so a sampled figure would make the bike-lane result look better or worse depending on the random seed. Summing per-trip risk gives the unbiased estimate without the variance — which is why 31% reads as 31% every time.

Why MySQL and not MongoDB?

Trips, vehicles and incidents are transactional records with real constraints — a trip cannot reference a vehicle that does not exist. That is relational. MySQL 8.4 also gives spatial types with SRID 4326 and spatial indexes, which every map and proximity feature depends on.

Why three services?

Express owns identity, transactions and the public contract, and is the only writer to MySQL. FastAPI owns everything numeric and holds no database credentials at all — every request carries the data it needs, which is what keeps it stateless. The browser never talks to the Python service.

Why isn't it finished?

Do not apologise. “Eleven of sixteen modules. The ones built carry the argument — forecast, simulate, optimise, measure. The five I left are mostly CRUD over a schema that already exists: parking, notifications, billing, emergency response. Each has a note in the module document saying it was skipped and why, so nothing looks forgotten.”

What would you do next?

One specific answer, not a list: “Charging infrastructure planning. The simulator already told us every unmet trip is a flat battery, not a missing vehicle — so the next module decides where charging points go, and the simulator is how I'd evaluate it.” That shows the system produced a finding that drives the roadmap.

If it breaks

First rule

Keep talking. Silence while you debug feels ten times longer than it is. Narrate what you expect to see and fix it while you speak — composure is marked at least as heavily as uptime.

If the whole thing will not start

Run npm run demo:check on screen. It prints eight checks and the exact command to fix any failure. Doing that in front of an examiner is not an admission of defeat — it shows the project has operational tooling, which most student projects do not.

The safety net

Live, Fleet and Admin need no Python service at all. If port 8000 refuses to cooperate, drop segments 2:30 and 7:30 and give the remaining time to Admin and Fleet. Say plainly that the routing service is not running on this machine and describe what it does. That is a far better look than fifteen minutes of debugging.
